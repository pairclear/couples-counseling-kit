# Using AI tools in couples work: a short practical guide

*Not legal advice. Check your professional code, your national regulator and data-protection law.*

## Good uses
- Drafting handouts and homework on general topics, then checking them yourself.
- Making a text plainer, shorter or translated.
- Practising skills with an invented couple.
- Structuring your own de-identified notes, if your rules allow it.

## Avoid
- Pasting identifiable client information into consumer AI chats.
- Asking AI to assess risk, diagnose, or decide on a treatment plan.
- Letting clients' messages be answered by AI without their knowledge.
- Citations or statistics from AI that you haven't checked.

## Data protection basics (EU)
- Information about mental health and sex life is special-category data under GDPR, Art. 9.
- If an AI service processes client data for you, you need a legal basis, a data-processing agreement and an assessment of where the data goes.
- De-identifying (removing names) is not the same as anonymising; unusual details can still identify people.

## A consent paragraph you can adapt

> I sometimes use software tools, including AI-based tools, to help me prepare general materials such as handouts. I do not enter your name or details that could identify you into these tools without your written consent. You can ask me at any time how I keep your information and you can say no to any use of such tools in your case.

## Couples-specific safety
- Screen for violence and coercive control individually before joint work (see [the intake template](../templates/intake-couple.md)).
- Do not share one partner's private disclosure about safety with the other.
- Keep a list of local domestic-violence and crisis services. A starting point: [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) and the [crisis list in not-a-therapist](https://github.com/pairclear/not-a-therapist/blob/main/data/crisis-resources.md).
